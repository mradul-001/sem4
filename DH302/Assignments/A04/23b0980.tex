\section*{Question 01}
Department of Health of Maharashtra Government plans to expand its telemedicine
services to underserved rural communities. Before proceeding, the director
needs
to conduct a comprehensive analysis to identify the best technique that could
influence the implementation plan.
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(A)} Which framework would be most appropriate for conducting
this
analysis
(Select only one)? \\[0.2cm]
\noindent PESTEL
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(B)} Provide suitable justification to support the selection
of answer. \\[0.2cm]
\noindent
PESTEL is best here because it looks at all the big things that can affect a
government plan — like rules (legal), money (economic), people’s habits
(social), and available technology. Since the health department wants to bring
telemedicine to underserved villages, they need to think about these bigger
outside
factors. PESTEL helps them see the full picture before starting.

\section*{Question 02}
Consider the following hypothesis: "A school-based obesity prevention program
will
be successful if it reaches a high proportion of at-risk students, effectively
reduces
BMI, is adopted by multiple schools, is implemented with fidelity across
different
settings, and produces sustainable behavioural changes over time."
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(A)} Which research framework does this hypothesis most
closely align with? \\[0.2cm]
\noindent RE-AIM
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(B)} Provide suitable justification to support the selection
of answer. \\[0.2cm]
\noindent
RE-AIM stands for Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, and
Maintenance. The hypothesis talks about reaching many students, reducing BMI
(effectiveness), being used in many schools (adoption), doing the program
properly in different places (implementation), and keeping the changes for a
long time (maintenance). This makes RE-AIM a good fit.

\section*{Question 03}
A public health department is planning a county-wide screening program for
hypertension. Based on previous screening initiatives, they expect that
approximately 30\% of adults in the county have hypertension (either diagnosed
or undiagnosed). The department wants to estimate the true prevalence of
hypertension with a margin of error of ±3 percentage points at a 95\%
confidence
level.
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(A)} Which of the following best describes how the required
sample size would
change if the expected prevalence of hypertension was actually 20\% instead of
30\% (keeping the same margin of error and confidence level)?\\[0.2cm]
\noindent The required sample size would decrease.
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(B)} Provide suitable justification to support the selection
of answer. \\[0.2cm]
\noindent
Sample size depends partly on how close the expected percentage is to 50\%.
When the expected prevalence is 30\%, it's closer to 50\% than 20\% is. Closer
to 50\% means more variation, so we need a bigger sample. If the true value is
actually 20\%, there’s less variation, so we don’t need as many people to get a
good estimate — meaning a smaller sample size is enough.

\section*{Question 04}
In 1954, Dr. Jonas Salk led one of the largest medical field trials in history
to test
his inactivated polio vaccine. The trial included 1.8 million children, with
approximately 440,000 receiving the vaccine, 210,000 receiving a placebo, and
1.2 million serving as observed controls. Among the vaccinated group, 33 cases
of polio were reported (0.0075\%), while the placebo group had 115 cases
(0.055\%). A modern pharmaceutical company is developing a new vaccine for an
emerging infectious disease with similar characteristics to polio. They plan to
conduct clinical trials with the following parameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Estimated disease incidence in the population: 0.05\%
    \item Estimated disease incidence in the population: 0.05\%
    \item Planned trial size: 15,000 participants (7,500 vaccine, 7,500
          placebo)
    \item Trial duration: 6 months
\end{itemize}
Evaluate whether the planned sample size and trial duration are sufficient to
justify the safety and efficacy of the new vaccine.
\\\\

\noindent \textbf{(A)} Calculate the expected number of cases in each group
based on the given
parameters. \\[0.2cm]
\noindent Expected number of cases in Vaccine group = 0.05\% $\times$ (1 -
0.85) $\times$
7500 = 0.5625\\
Expected number of cases in Placebo group = 0.05\% $\times$ 7500 =
3.75\\[0.1cm]
\noindent The disease incidence in case of vaccinated group reduced by a factor
of 0.15
because of the efficacy of the vaccine.
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(B)} Discuss at least two statistical and ethical
considerations related to
sample size adequacy for establishing vaccine safety. \\[0.2cm]
\textbf{1. Too few cases expected:}\\
Only ~4 in placebo and ~1 in vaccine : very small difference.
Unlike Dr. Salk's trial (33 vs. 115), the differences between the number of
cases is very less, making it hard to draw any statistical conclusion.\\
\textbf{2. Disobedience Bias:}\\
If participants don’t follow protocol, the small number of cases becomes even
less meaningful.
\\\\
\noindent \textbf{(C)} Recommend any modifications to the study design to
better justify safety,
referring to relevant statistical principles and historical precedents like
the Salk vaccine trial. \\[0.2cm]
Modifications which can be done are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We can increase the sample size, maybe like Salk's trial (~1.8
          million) or at
          least 100,000+ to detect both rare side effects and get reliable
          efficacy
          estimate.
    \item Extend trial duration, e.g., to 1 year, so more disease cases appear
          and trends are clearer.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Question 05}

\noindent \textbf{(A)} Develop an appropriate hypothesis to test the
effectiveness of the PoshanShakti program in reducing malnutrition in rural
Indian schoolchildren.
\\[0.2cm]
The PoshanShakti program leads to greater improvements in children's
nutritional status compared to the standard Mid-Day Meal scheme in rural Indian
schools.
\\\\
\noindent\textbf{(B)} Which research framework (PICOT, PICO, PEO, PESTEL, or
RE-AIM) would be
most appropriate for this study? Justify your selection and explain how the
elements of your chosen framework would apply to this specific case.
\\[0.2cm]
\textbf{RE-AIM}\\
This framework evaluates health interventions across Reach, Effectiveness,
Adoption,
Implementation, and Maintenance — all crucial for implementation of a public
program like
PoshanShakti.
\\\\
\noindent\textbf{(C)} Recommend three specific data visualizations (charts or
plots) that would
effectively communicate the program's outcomes to Indian policymakers and
stakeholders. For each, explain what variables would be plotted and how the
visualization would help stakeholders understand the results.
\\[0.2cm]
Some visualizations that would help to communicate the program's outcome are as
follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bar Chart:} A bar chart can be used to compare underweight
          rates in both groups. This would highlight the nutritional
          improvements from
          the PoshanShakti program.
    \item \textbf{Line Graph:} A line graph showing monthly attendance trends
          would show how the program affects the attendance rates of the
          students.
    \item \textbf{Cost-effectiveness Scatter Plot:} A scatter plot of health
          improvement versus cost per student would help evaluate how cost
          effective the
          program is.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{(F)}\\
i) Identify two potential confounding variables that might affect the
evaluation of
this nutrition program in rural India.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Food at home:}
          Children's nutritional status is influenced not only by school meals
          but
          also by the quantity and quality of food they receive at home. If
          families in
          the intervention group have better access to nutritious food at home
          compared
          to those in the control group, the observed improvements might not be
          solely
          due to the PoshanShakti program.
    \item \textbf{Access to local healthcare services:}
          Regions with better functioning Primary Health Centers (PHCs) may
          offer
          additional support such as supplements or general healthcare that
          could enhance
          children's growth. If more intervention schools are located in areas
          with
          better health infrastructure, it could impact the results of the
          program.
\end{itemize}
ii) For each confounding variable, explain how it might influence the results
and
propose a specific strategy to control for or mitigate its effects in the study
design or analysis, considering the practical constraints of conducting
research
in rural India.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Access to local healthcare services:}
          Collect data on household food availability and quality through
          surveys and control for it using statistical methods.
    \item \textbf{Access to local healthcare services:}
    We can note how close the nearby health centers are for each school. Then, while analyzing the results, we can consider this factor to make sure it doesn't affect the outcome unfairly. Another way is to choose control schools that have similar healthcare access as the intervention schools, so the comparison stays fair.
\end{itemize}
